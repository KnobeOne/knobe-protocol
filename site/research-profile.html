A use to pilot: social research

What if the participant kept the original?

Today the researcher holds the data, and the participant holds a photocopy of a consent form. This page describes the inversion: the participant keeps the sealed original, and researchers work from derivatives whose lineage points back to it, with the consent terms traveling inside every copy.

Status, plainly: every mechanism on this page works today and can be verified here, in your browser. No study has yet been run this way. This is a workflow we think deserves piloting, and an invitation to pilot it.

What breaks in research today

The failure on this site's front page is a research scenario: an oral history whose consent terms, do-not-quote request, and contextual notes fall away as an excerpt moves through an AI abstract into a newsletter and a grant report. Every step was reasonable. By the fourth, the words still circulate and the conditions that governed them are gone.

Three research-specific problems sit underneath that decay. Consent detaches from data: the terms live in a filing cabinet while the transcript travels. Custody is one-sided: participants surrender their words and keep nothing that proves what they agreed to. Derivation is invisible: by the time a quote reaches print, no one can trace it back through coding, translation, and summarization to the consented original.

None of this requires new infrastructure to fix. It requires the consent terms and the lineage to live inside the file. That part exists.

The custody inversion, step by step

Here is the lifecycle we propose. Each mechanism in it (sealing, hashing, lineage, local verification) is part of the frozen protocol and runs in the tooling on this site. What has not happened yet is a study conducted this way.

  1. An interview is recorded and transcribed, as it is now.
  2. The transcript is sealed as a .knobe.md: the words as the readable body, the consent terms inside the sealed payload.
  3. The participant receives the sealed original. It is plain text; it lives in their email or their drive, opens in any editor, and verifies locally years later, with no account and no platform.
  4. The researcher works from derivatives: a de-identified excerpt, a coded segment, a translation. Each is a new sealed object whose parents field chains to the original's hash and whose content_type declares the transformation.
  5. A publication quotes the derivative. The chain from consent terms to printed quotation is inspectable end to end, by an IRB or by anyone holding the files.
  6. Years later, the participant grants a new use by issuing a new license object chained to the original. Or declines. The original never left their hands.
  7. Anyone holding the objects can verify the whole chain with a local verifier. No registry, no platform, no one's permission.

What do "consent terms inside the payload" look like? These are the actual fields from the sealed example below, not a mockup:

"research:consent-version": "illustrative-1.0", "research:permitted-uses": ["quotation-with-pseudonym-and-year", "educational-display", "verification-demo"], "research:prohibited-uses": ["re-identification", "commercial-reuse"], "research:custody": "participant retains the sealed original; researcher copies are derivatives with lineage"

What the protocol does here, and what it deliberately does not. KNOBE makes consent terms legible, citable, and refusable at the point of use. A cooperating tool or agent can check an object's declared conditions before acting, and decline with a citable basis. The open protocol does not process payments, verify legal identity, or enforce anything against an uncooperative party. Markets, identity, and enforcement are application-layer work for credentialed environments, not properties of the file format.

Where else this could land

Qualitative and archival

  • Oral history and archives. Do-not-quote requests and context notes would travel with every excerpt into every future finding aid.
  • Ethnography and community-based research. Informant-held originals; communities that hold the corpus and license researcher access, rather than the reverse.
  • Qualitative coding. Codebooks as sealed objects; every coded excerpt chains to its raw source and codebook version.
  • Translation. Translated instruments would carry their own equivalence caveats as fidelity limits.

Quantitative and replication

  • Replication packages and meta-analysis. Every extracted estimate chains to the exact source object it came from.
  • Data donation. Donors would keep sealed originals and issue purpose-limited licenses instead of surrendering raw exports.
  • Synthetic-respondent hygiene. Declared-synthetic objects keep honest simulation studies separable from contaminated human panels.

Hard questions, answered plainly

Can a participant withdraw?
Custody inversion helps: the participant deletes their original, and a revocation object chained to it makes withdrawal machine-legible, so cooperating tools decline further use and cite it. No protocol on earth claws back copies already made. KNOBE makes withdrawal visible and citable, not magically enforced.
Does the seal prove when something was created?
No. created_date is a declared field; the seal proves content, not time. Preregistration and priority claims need trusted timestamping, which is application-layer work (for example, ledger anchoring in a credentialed environment) and never a requirement of the open format.
Does this enforce consent?
No, and this page should be the first to say so. KNOBE makes the terms travel with the object and be checkable at the moment of use. Cooperating software honors them with a citable basis; a hostile actor can ignore any file format. Enforcement belongs to institutions and credentialed platforms.
What about re-identification?
A sealed pseudonymous object is still a disclosure-risk object; sealing does not anonymize. De-identification remains the researcher's methodological responsibility. What KNOBE adds is that the de-identified derivative declares its transformation and chains to a consented source an IRB can audit.

At larger scale: a study we have not run

The inversion works at any scale. A fifteen-participant qualitative study is a complete use, not a stepping stone. What follows is the far end of the range, described in the conditional, because it should be.

In research on what AI does to cognition and culture, the stimulus and the response both need provenance, and human-AI interaction is currently the least-provenanced data in science. A panel study built on the inversion would look like this: participants donate sealed monthly slices of their AI interactions. Each donation records which model produced the stimulus, what the human did with it, and the participant's terms. Each participant keeps their own longitudinal corpus, so the panel's value accrues to the panelists and not only to the lab.

  • Contamination would be inspectable by declaration. Every object states its AI involvement, so corpora could be built AI-labeled or AI-excluded without relying on detection heuristics.
  • Replication would inherit materials, not methods paragraphs. Instrument, exact stimuli, and responses, all sealed and byte-verifiable years later.
  • Waves could not be quietly revised. Wave-one objects stay sealed against a frozen specification.
  • Analysis tools would consult each object's terms before use, honoring aggregate-only conditions per object, per participant, with a citable basis for every refusal.

This is the study we would most like to run with partners. We have not run it. That distinction is the point of this page.

For your IRB

KNOBE does not change what you collect or how you collect it. It changes how data is stored and transmitted: a plain-text file with the consent terms embedded, instead of a transcript in one folder and a consent form in a drawer. Integrity can be checked in a browser with the Lens, with no technical training. Whether sealing counts as a protocol amendment is your board's call; for pilot partners, we will draft the protocol language together. And sealing is not anonymization; de-identification remains a methods responsibility, as stated above.

A coda, past the edge of this page: the participant who keeps their interviews is the same person as the student who keeps their learning record and anyone who keeps their AI conversations. A lifetime of sealed objects is a personal corpus, and platforms become interchangeable lenses over it. That argument is larger than research and belongs to the white paper.

We are looking for co-investigators, not test subjects. A pilot can be as small as sealing one existing, consented dataset and evaluating the workflow, or as large as writing the inversion into a new IRB protocol.
